## Simple Summary

Sweep residual LP and Vault balances out of deprecated Thales LPs, Overtime V1 LPs, Thales Vaults, and Overtime V1 Vaults — totaling approximately $53,743 across Optimism, Arbitrum, and Base — into per-chain Merkle-root claim contracts. Every dormant LP retains a permanent, gas-efficient right to claim their full pro-rata share. The protocol retires four classes of unmaintained contracts and removes their long-tail exploit surface.

## Abstract

The deprecated Thales LPs, Overtime V1 LPs, Thales Vaults, and Overtime V1 Vaults visible in the protocol dashboard collectively hold approximately $53,743 across multiple deployments per LP/Vault series and three chains. All rounds are ended; allocations are static; utilization rates against active markets are effectively zero. The funds belong to LPs who have, for periods spanning multiple rounds and in some cases multiple years, declined to withdraw — likely a mix of forgotten wallets, deeply underwater positions written off psychologically, dust balances not worth gas, and lost keys.

These contracts are no longer part of the active codebase, are no longer covered by ongoing audits or bug bounties at the same priority as live infrastructure, and present a passive long-tail risk surface. As automated vulnerability discovery improves — including LLM-assisted static analysis and fuzzing pipelines targeting historical DeFi deployments — the expected cost of leaving dormant balances in unmaintained code rises monotonically.

This proposal authorizes the pDAO multisig to:

1. Snapshot LP and Vault balances at a defined block per chain.
2. Where existing contract methods permit, invoke owner/admin withdrawal paths to move funds to a pDAO-controlled treasury address.
3. Where existing contracts do not expose a suitable method, propose and execute a minimal upgrade strictly limited to adding a balance-sweep function with a hard-coded destination.
4. Deploy three per-chain `OvertimeLegacyClaim` contracts (Optimism, Arbitrum, Base) holding the swept funds and a Merkle root of `(claimant, token, amount)` tuples.
5. Publish the snapshot data, Merkle tree, and per-address claim portal indefinitely.

Claims remain open in perpetuity with no expiry. No LP loses any economic claim. The protocol reduces its passive attack surface from four contract families across three chains to one audited claim contract per chain.

## Motivation

### 1. Passive Exploit Risk Is Asymmetric and Compounding

The deprecated contracts in question have not received feature changes in many rounds — Thales LP rounds at #81, #143, #136, #113; Overtime V1 Sports/Parlay LP rounds at #99, #72, #85, #73, #57, #59; Thales Vault rounds in the #122–#142 range; Overtime V1 Vault rounds in the #120–#144 range. Each represents a deployment frozen at a codebase that is no longer the locus of security attention.

Three concrete risk vectors:

- **Dependency rot.** Many of these contracts integrate with external price feeds, oracle adapters, and ERC-20 implementations that have themselves been upgraded or deprecated. A change in a dependency's behavior (e.g. an oracle returning stale data, a wrapped-asset contract changing pause semantics) can create unanticipated state transitions in code no one is actively monitoring.
- **Newly disclosed primitive vulnerabilities.** Reentrancy variants, signature-malleability patterns, and proxy-storage-collision bugs continue to be discovered in classes of contracts deployed years before disclosure. Live contracts get patched; deprecated ones do not.
- **Automated discovery scaling.** Adversarial tooling — including ML-assisted symbolic execution and LLM-driven vulnerability hunting against verified bytecode — lowers the marginal cost of finding bugs in obscure or low-TVL contracts. The historical assumption that "$5k–$30k pools are not worth attacking" weakens when discovery is near-free and exploits can be batched.

The asymmetry is the key argument. Cost of a sweep-and-migrate operation: one-time engineering, audit, and gas spend, bounded and measurable. Cost of an exploit on a deprecated contract: total loss of remaining balance plus reputational damage to Overtime regardless of the contract's deprecated status, because users and press will not distinguish "active Overtime V2" from "deprecated Thales V1" in headlines.

### 2. Capital Currently Earns Nothing for LPs

Every deprecated entry on the dashboard shows `0.00% Round PnL` and rounds that have ended. The dormant LPs are not earning yield; they are not providing liquidity; they are simply parked. Returning the funds to an explicit claim mechanism is strictly better for the LP than leaving them in a contract that no longer transacts.

### 3. Operational Burden of Maintaining Multiple Deprecated UIs

The current dashboard surface area includes four legacy product lines across three chains, each with its own withdrawal flow, round mechanics, and edge cases. A single per-chain claim contract collapses this to one supported flow, reducing support load, frontend maintenance, and the surface where a UI bug could cause a user to interact incorrectly with a contract.

### 4. The "Just Leave It" Counterargument

The strongest objection is to do nothing: contracts have been quiet for many rounds; LPs who want to withdraw can already do so; introducing a migration introduces both technical risk and governance optics risk. This objection has weight and is addressed in the Rationale section. In summary: doing nothing is a bet that no exploit will materialize before LPs voluntarily withdraw on their own timeline, which for some addresses appears to be "never." The proposal trades a small, controlled, audited migration risk now for the removal of an open-ended risk.

## Specification

### 1. Scope of Migration

The following contract families are in scope, per the protocol dashboard:

- **Thales LPs**: Optimism (Thales LP, Thales LP sUSD), Arbitrum (Thales LP), Base (Thales LP).
- **Overtime V1 LPs**: Optimism (Sports LP, Parlay LP), Arbitrum (Sports LP, Parlay LP), Base (Sports LP, Parlay LP).
- **Thales Vaults**: Optimism (Discount, Degen Discount, Safu Discount), Arbitrum (Discount, Degen Discount, Safu Discount).
- **Overtime V1 Vaults**: Optimism (Discount, Degen Discount, Safu Discount, Upsettoor).

Any other deprecated contract holding LP positions and discovered during snapshot preparation will be added to this list via a minor amendment before execution, subject to pDAO confirmation.

### 2. Snapshot

- A snapshot block per chain will be selected and announced no less than 14 days in advance.
- At the snapshot block, balances of every LP / vault depositor will be read directly from contract state — `balanceOf`, `getBalanceOf`, `userVault`, or equivalent per-contract accessor.
- The raw snapshot data (chain, contract address, claimant address, token address, amount) will be published as a CSV and as a Merkle tree, with the Merkle root committed onchain in the claim contract constructor.
- Snapshot data and Merkle proofs will be hosted in the official Overtime GitHub repository and IPFS-pinned. Frontend will compute proofs locally; no trusted backend.

### 3. Withdrawal Mechanism Per Contract

For each in-scope contract, the engineering team will produce a table classifying it as one of:

- **(A) Owner-withdrawable via existing method.** Use existing function. No upgrade required. Lowest risk.
- **(B) Owner-withdrawable only via contract upgrade.** Propose minimal upgrade adding a single function `sweepDormant(address[] calldata depositors, address destination)` or equivalent, restricted to owner, restricted to post-round-ended state, restricted to a one-time call per round, and with explicit per-depositor amount accounting. Upgrade ABI and code diff to be published and audited before deployment.
- **(C) Not upgradeable / immutable.** Funds stay in original contract. Users must withdraw using existing flows. These are excluded from the migration and flagged in the claim portal as "withdraw from original contract."

The classification table is part of the proposal artifact and must be published for review during the discussion phase, not introduced at execution time.

### 4. Claim Contract Design

Three per-chain `OvertimeLegacyClaim` contracts (Optimism, Arbitrum, Base). Per-chain rather than cross-chain to avoid bridge risk; the original funds are already on those chains and there is no upside to consolidating.

Each claim contract:

- Holds the swept funds in their original tokens (sUSD, USDC, THALES, OVER, and any other relevant ERC-20s).
- Stores a single Merkle root committed at deployment.
- Exposes `claim(address token, uint256 amount, bytes32[] calldata proof)` callable by the leaf's claimant address.
- Supports batched claims via `claimMany(...)` for users with multiple positions on the same chain.
- No expiry. No reclamation by treasury. No admin function to alter the Merkle root post-deployment. The contract is intentionally minimal and immutable to satisfy the proposal's own risk-reduction motivation.
- Emits `Claimed(address claimant, address token, uint256 amount)` for offchain indexing.

Codebase: single Solidity file, ideally under 200 lines, single audit pass by a tier-1 firm before deployment. Auditing cost is bounded; this is not a complex contract.

### 5. Dust Policy

Balances below a per-token dust threshold (denominated in USD-equivalent at snapshot, e.g. $1) remain claimable but the proposal explicitly acknowledges that gas costs will exceed the claim value for most users. Dust is not aggregated, swept, or donated; it sits in the claim contract indefinitely. This avoids any accusation of "the DAO took my $0.87."

### 6. Risk Controls During Migration

- Multisig with timelock on the sweep transactions per chain. No single signer can execute.
- Per-chain sweep executed only after the claim contract on that chain is deployed, audited, and funded with the Merkle root.
- 7-day delay between Merkle root publication and the first claim contract deployment, allowing public verification that every depositor's leaf is present and correct.
- Post-sweep verification: total swept funds must equal sum of all leaves in the Merkle tree, within a defined tolerance for rounding. Discrepancies block the deployment of the corresponding claim contract until resolved.

### 7. Communications

- Forum post 21 days before snapshot.
- Twitter, Discord, and email-via-frontend (if Overtime collects email) notification 14 days before snapshot.
- Claim portal at a stable URL with wallet-connect, automatic proof generation, and per-address claim summary across all three chains.
- Indefinite uptime commitment for the portal, with portal source code mirrored to IPFS so it remains accessible even if the official hosting lapses.

## Rationale

### Why Not Leave Funds Where They Are

This is the central objection and deserves direct treatment.

Doing nothing relies on three assumptions: (a) deprecated contracts will not be exploited; (b) LPs who want their funds will withdraw on their own; (c) the protocol bears no responsibility for losses in deprecated contracts. Each is weaker than it appears:

- (a) is a tail-risk bet, and tail risks compound with time and with improvements in adversarial tooling.
- (b) is empirically false. Rounds have been ended for extended periods and these balances remain. The revealed preference is that some non-trivial fraction of LPs will never withdraw absent active prompting.
- (c) is legally and reputationally not how users perceive it. An exploit on a contract bearing the Thales or Overtime name harms the active protocol's standing regardless of deprecated status.

The migration converts an unbounded, unmonitored risk into a bounded, audited one-time operation.

### Why a New Contract Instead of Indefinite Status Quo

A single audited claim contract per chain has a smaller and more characterized attack surface than four contract families per chain. The new contract has no betting logic, no oracle dependencies, no round mechanics, no AMM math — it has token transfers gated by a Merkle proof. This is among the simplest possible contract designs and has many battle-tested reference implementations (e.g. Uniswap MerkleDistributor, used as the basis for hundreds of airdrops without incident).

### Why Not Donate Unclaimed Funds to Treasury After N Years

Tempting and explicitly rejected. The motivation of this proposal is to protect LPs, not to reclaim their assets. Introducing any reclamation timeline reframes the proposal as a soft confiscation and would justifiably attract opposition. Funds remain claimable forever.

### Why Per-Chain Rather Than Consolidated

Bridge risk is the single largest source of recent DeFi exploit losses. Consolidating funds to one chain would force every LP to interact with a bridge, expose pooled funds to bridge custodianship during transit, and introduce a chain-of-trust dependency. Per-chain claim contracts preserve the existing chain assignment of each LP's funds and require no bridge interaction.

### Why Merkle Rather Than On-Chain List

A Merkle root commits to thousands of (claimant, token, amount) tuples in one 32-byte hash. Storing the full list onchain is gas-prohibitive at deployment and not necessary — proofs are cheap to verify and the full list lives offchain on IPFS and GitHub for audit.

## Implementation

1. **Discovery and Classification (week 1–2)**
   - Enumerate every in-scope contract address per chain.
   - Classify each as (A), (B), or (C) per Specification §3.
   - Publish classification table to forum.

2. **Snapshot Tooling (week 2–3)**
   - Script to read per-contract depositor balances at a target block.
   - Script to construct Merkle tree and proofs.
   - Both scripts open-source, with deterministic output verifiable by any third party.

3. **Upgrade Drafts for Type-B Contracts (week 2–4)**
   - Minimal `sweepDormant` patches per contract requiring upgrade.
   - Internal review and external audit.

4. **Claim Contract Development (week 3–5)**
   - Single Solidity implementation, deployed per chain.
   - Audit pass.

5. **Snapshot Block Selection and Announcement (week 5)**
   - Public announcement, 14-day notice.

6. **Snapshot Execution (week 7)**
   - Snapshot taken; Merkle root published.
   - 7-day public verification window.

7. **Sweep and Claim Contract Deployment (week 8)**
   - Execute upgrades and sweeps via multisig.
   - Deploy and fund claim contracts.
   - Verify funded amounts match Merkle tree sums.

8. **Portal Launch (week 8)**
   - Claim portal live.
   - Announcement across all channels.

9. **Long-Term Maintenance**
   - Portal source code mirrored to IPFS.
   - Annual reminder communications to non-claimed addresses (where reachable via onchain heuristics or original deposit-frontend cookies if applicable).

## Conclusion

The deprecated Thales LPs, Overtime V1 LPs, Thales Vaults, and Overtime V1 Vaults are no longer active components of Overtime's infrastructure but continue to hold LP funds. Their continued existence is a passive risk surface that grows monotonically more attractive to automated adversarial tooling as time passes, with no offsetting benefit to the LPs whose funds they hold.

This proposal authorizes a bounded, audited migration of those funds to per-chain Merkle-root claim contracts, preserving every LP's full economic claim, removing the deprecated contracts from the protocol's exposed surface, and consolidating four legacy support flows into one supported claim flow. No funds are confiscated, no claim expires, and no chain bridge is introduced.

The alternative — doing nothing — is a bet that no exploit will be discovered against unmaintained code over an indefinite horizon. That is not a bet the protocol should be implicitly taking on behalf of its LPs.
