## Simple Summary

This OIP proposes raising the **SafeBox fee to 2%** and the **LP performance fee to 50%**, while granting the protocol DAO (pDAO) discretion to set either parameter at any value at or below those maximums. The 2% SafeBox fee and 50% LP performance fee act as ceilings, not fixed values, and pDAO may adjust them downward based on pool performance, ongoing sports activity, and other relevant factors. Any change to these parameters must be made prior to a new round starting.

## Abstract

Overtime's liquidity pools have been performing well. At the same time, the protocol directs all of its profits toward buyback and burn of the $OVER token, while bearing the full set of risks required to operate the pools — covering potential exploits, oracle failures, and other non-organic losses, and acting as backstop to ensure LP performance and a healthy market-making capacity.

This combination means the protocol carries significant risk and reinvests its earnings entirely into the token, yet captures a relatively modest share of the value the pools generate. To ensure the long-term durability of the protocol and its ability to absorb these risks, protocol revenue needs to be higher.

This OIP therefore proposes to:

- Set the **SafeBox fee** at a maximum of **2%**.
- Set the **LP performance fee** at a maximum of **50%** of profits from each positive (profitable) LP round.
- Grant pDAO discretion to set both parameters at any value at or below these maximums.
- Require that any change to these parameters takes effect only prior to the start of a new round.

The SafeBox fee currently stands at 2% historically (per TIP-163), and the LP performance fee currently stands at 30% (per TIP-233 and TIP-234). This OIP confirms the 2% SafeBox fee as the maximum and raises the LP performance fee ceiling from 30% to 50%. It also supersedes the direction proposed in OIP-255, which sought to reduce the SafeBox fee to 1%; given improved pool performance and the protocol's risk and revenue profile, maintaining the SafeBox fee at up to 2% is warranted.

## Motivation

The protocol's liquidity pools are the backbone of Overtime. They make markets, take on user action, and generate the trading activity the protocol depends on. Recent performance has been strong, and the pools are operating in a healthy state.

However, the economics around the pools are asymmetric in a way that disadvantages the protocol:

- **The protocol uses all of its profits for buyback and burn.** Protocol revenue is not retained as a growing operational or insurance buffer; it is continuously reinvested into the $OVER token via buyback and burn. This benefits token holders but leaves the protocol with limited retained earnings to draw on.
- **The protocol takes on all of the risk.** The protocol underwrites the risks of operating the pools — potential exploits, oracle errors, latency and volatility exposure, and other non-organic losses — and acts as the backstop that ensures LP performance and the continued availability of competitive liquidity. Liquidity providers benefit from this protection, but the cost and tail risk of providing it sit with the protocol.

Given that the protocol both reinvests all of its profits and absorbs all of the downside risk, its share of the upside should be commensurate. A higher SafeBox fee and a higher LP performance fee increase protocol revenue, strengthening its ability to cover exploits and losses, sustain buyback and burn, and ensure durable LP performance over time.

Because conditions change — pool performance varies, sports calendars ebb and flow, and competitive dynamics shift — these parameters should not be rigidly fixed at their maximums. Instead, pDAO should hold discretion to set them at or below the maximums based on real conditions, so that the protocol can capture appropriate revenue when conditions allow while remaining competitive and supportive of LPs when conditions call for it.

## Specification

This OIP entails the protocol DAO (pDAO) adjusting two parameters:

1. The **SafeBox fee** is set to a maximum of **2%**, applied uniformly to volume across all products.
2. The **LP performance fee** is set to a maximum of **50%** of profits from each positive (profitable) LP round, across all liquidity pools.
3. pDAO holds discretion to set the SafeBox fee at any value at or below **2%**.
4. pDAO holds discretion to set the LP performance fee at any value at or below **50%**.
5. pDAO may exercise this discretion based on the performance of the pools, ongoing sports activity, and other relevant factors affecting protocol durability and competitiveness.
6. Any change to either parameter must be made **prior to a new round starting**, so that no change affects a round that is already in progress.
7. pDAO is entitled to set and adjust these parameters within the bounds defined above on an ad hoc basis. $OVER holders retain ultimate authority over these parameters through the standard governance process (GitHub proposal, Discord discussion, Snapshot vote), and may by Snapshot vote override pDAO decisions or amend the maximums set by this OIP.

For the avoidance of doubt, the 2% SafeBox fee and the 50% LP performance fee are ceilings. They define the maximum values pDAO may set; they are not minimums and not fixed values. pDAO may set lower values and adjust them over time within these bounds.

## Rationale

The pools are performing well, which makes this an appropriate time to align the protocol's revenue share with the risk it carries and the profits it reinvests.

Setting maximums rather than fixed values keeps governance lightweight while preserving flexibility. A single fixed fee cannot remain optimal across changing pool performance and seasonal swings in sports activity. By defining 2% and 50% as ceilings and delegating in-bounds adjustment to pDAO, the protocol can respond to conditions without a new OIP for every change, while the ceilings preserve a clear, governance-set limit on how much can be extracted.

Requiring changes to take effect only before a new round starts protects liquidity providers from mid-round surprises. LPs commit capital to a round under a known fee structure; changing the rules mid-round would undermine that commitment and predictability. Aligning parameter changes to round boundaries keeps the system fair and transparent.

Raising the LP performance fee ceiling from 30% to 50% shifts more of the upside from profitable rounds to the protocol, which is the party absorbing the downside and reinvesting profits into buyback and burn. Confirming the SafeBox fee at up to 2% — rather than reducing it to 1% as contemplated in OIP-255 — reflects the improved performance of the pools and the protocol's need for higher revenue to ensure durability.

This approach prioritizes long-term protocol viability. Higher, risk-aligned revenue strengthens the protocol's capacity to cover exploits and losses, sustain buyback and burn, and keep liquidity healthy, while pDAO discretion ensures the protocol stays competitive and responsive to changing conditions.

## Test Cases

- pDAO can set the SafeBox fee to any value at or below 2%; values above 2% are not permitted.
- pDAO can set the LP performance fee to any value at or below 50%; values above 50% are not permitted.
- A parameter change configured before a round starts applies to that round; no change applies to a round already in progress.
- Profitable rounds allocate the configured LP performance fee (up to 50%) to the protocol.
- $OVER holders can, via Snapshot vote, override a pDAO parameter setting or amend the maximums defined in this OIP.

## Implementation

- Update the SafeBox fee parameter to the configured value (maximum 2%) via governance-controlled configuration.
- Update the LP performance fee variable to the configured value (maximum 50%) via governance-controlled configuration.
- Ensure parameter changes are scheduled and applied prior to the start of a new round.
- Document the maximums and the pDAO discretion process in public governance documentation.

All changes are parameter-level and do not require contract migrations, ticket invalidation, or user-side changes.
