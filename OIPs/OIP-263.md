## Simple Summary

This OIP proposes dissolving the Overtime Council and moving Overtime governance to direct Snapshot voting by \$OVER holders.

## Abstract

The Overtime Council is currently a delegated governance body elected by \$OVER holders. The Council consists of 5 members and decides protocol proposals by council vote, with regular proposals requiring a 3 out of 5 majority and meta-governance proposals requiring a higher threshold.

This model served Thales, and later Overtime, through roughly five years of protocol growth, product iteration, and mostly bear-market conditions. It provided fast, delegated decision-making during a period when the protocol required frequent technical, economic, and governance changes.

As Overtime has matured, the need for frequent governance intervention has declined. Previous governance changes already reflected this trend: council terms were adjusted, the Council was reduced from 7 members to 5, and Council stipends were reduced to better align governance costs with reduced governance activity.

This OIP proposes the next step in that evolution: dissolving the Overtime Council and moving to direct Snapshot voting by \$OVER holders.

Under the proposed system, anyone may still draft and discuss OIPs through the existing governance flow. The process remains: GitHub proposal, Discord discussion, then Snapshot vote. The key change is that final approval would come directly from \$OVER holders rather than elected Council members.

## Motivation

The Council model was appropriate when Thales was an early-stage protocol with a heavy technical roadmap, frequent deployments, token launch, staking, migration to Optimism, and later the growth of Overtime Markets. Delegated governance allowed the protocol to move quickly while still preserving tokenholder legitimacy.

That context has changed.

Overtime is now a more mature protocol with a clearer product focus, more stable operational structure, and lower frequency of major governance decisions. As proposal volume has decreased, the need for an elected Council to actively review and vote on every OIP has also decreased.

At the same time, Council engagement has naturally become less consistent. This is not unique to Overtime; broader crypto governance participation has generally softened. However, in practice, it can create unnecessary coordination overhead around proposal discussion and voting.

There is also a cost consideration. Council stipends made sense when the Council carried a more active operational governance burden. With lower proposal frequency and reduced need for delegated decision-making, continuing to compensate Council members in the same governance structure is no longer the most efficient use of protocol resources.

Direct Snapshot voting better reflects the current stage of Overtime governance. \$OVER holders should be able to vote directly on protocol changes, while the existing public proposal and discussion process preserves transparency and community review.

## Specification

This OIP entails dissolving the Overtime Council and replacing Council voting with direct Snapshot voting by \$OVER holders.

The proposed governance structure is:

1. Overtime Council is dissolved.
2. OIPs continue to follow the existing proposal flow:
    - GitHub proposal publication.
    - Discord discussion.
    - Snapshot vote.
3. Any community member may draft and propose an OIP.
4. If Snapshot posting requires privileged access, authorized team members or governance operators may post the vote on behalf of the proposer.
5. Voting power is based on \$OVER token holdings according to Snapshot configuration.
6. A minimum of 10,000,000 \$OVER voting power must participate for a vote to reach quorum.
7. Regular OIPs require at least 60% majority to pass.
8. Meta-governance OIPs require at least 80% majority to pass.
9. Council stipends are discontinued once the Council is dissolved.

For the avoidance of doubt, this OIP changes the approval mechanism for OIPs. It does not remove the existing public proposal and discussion process.

## Rationale

This proposal follows the same governance maturity path already reflected in earlier governance changes.

The Council was first useful because Thales was early, technically active, and required frequent protocol decisions. Later, as proposal volume decreased, the Council was reduced from 7 members to 5. Stipends were then reduced to reflect lower governance activity.

Moving to direct \$OVER-holder voting is the logical next step.

It improves governance by:

- Giving \$OVER holders direct decision-making power.
- Removing coordination friction around Council voting.
- Reducing unnecessary governance costs.
- Preserving the public OIP discussion process.
- Keeping higher thresholds for meta-governance changes.

The 10,000,000 \$OVER quorum requirement ensures that votes require meaningful participation. The 60% regular proposal threshold avoids simple narrow-majority capture, while the 80% meta-governance threshold preserves stronger protection for governance rule changes.

## Implementation

Implementation is expected to be governance and operations focused.

The governance operators should:

1. Update Snapshot governance settings to reflect direct \$OVER-holder voting.
2. Configure quorum at 10,000,000 \$OVER voting power.
3. Configure regular proposal approval at 60% majority.
4. Configure meta-governance approval at 80% majority.
5. Discontinue Overtime Council stipend payments.
6. Update public governance documentation to reflect the new process.
