## Simple Summary

This OIP proposes dissolving the Overtime Council and moving Overtime governance to direct Snapshot voting by $OVER holders.

## Abstract

The Overtime Council is currently a delegated governance body elected by $OVER holders. The Council consists of 5 members and decides protocol proposals by council vote, with regular proposals requiring a 3 out of 5 majority and meta-governance proposals requiring a higher threshold.

This model served Thales, and later Overtime, through roughly five years of protocol growth, product iteration, and mostly bear-market conditions. It provided fast, delegated decision-making during a period when the protocol required frequent technical, economic, and governance changes.

As Overtime has matured, the need for frequent governance intervention has declined. Previous governance changes already reflected this trend: council terms were adjusted, the Council was reduced from 7 members to 5, and Council stipends were reduced to better align governance costs with reduced governance activity.

This OIP proposes the next step in that evolution: dissolving the Overtime Council and moving to direct Snapshot voting by $OVER holders.

Under the proposed system, anyone may still draft and discuss OIPs through the existing governance flow. The process remains: GitHub proposal, Discord discussion, then Snapshot vote. The key change is that final approval would come directly from $OVER holders rather than elected Council members.

To address concerns raised during the OIP discussion, this proposal also introduces a limited Elder veto safeguard. The safeguard allows at least 3 former Overtime Council members, excluding active core contributors, to publicly veto a Snapshot vote in Discord before it is considered final. This creates a lightweight emergency review layer without preserving the current Council structure as the default approval body, while also ensuring the core team cannot block proposals single-handedly.

This OIP also clarifies that Overtime treasury-controlled $OVER is not eligible to vote on governance proposals. This applies to the Overtime treasury, treasury-associated wallets, and treasury-controlled multisigs. Direct governance should reflect the will of circulating $OVER holders, not protocol-controlled token balances.

## Motivation

The Council model was appropriate when Thales was an early-stage protocol with a heavy technical roadmap, frequent deployments, token launch, staking, migration to Optimism, and later the growth of Overtime Markets. Delegated governance allowed the protocol to move quickly while still preserving tokenholder legitimacy.

That context has changed.

Overtime is now a more mature protocol with a clearer product focus, more stable operational structure, and lower frequency of major governance decisions. As proposal volume has decreased, the need for an elected Council to actively review and vote on every OIP has also decreased.

At the same time, Council engagement has naturally become less consistent. This is not unique to Overtime; broader crypto governance participation has generally softened. However, in practice, it can create unnecessary coordination overhead around proposal discussion and voting.

There is also a cost consideration. Council stipends made sense when the Council carried a more active operational governance burden. With lower proposal frequency and reduced need for delegated decision-making, continuing to compensate Council members in the same governance structure is no longer the most efficient use of protocol resources.

Direct Snapshot voting better reflects the current stage of Overtime governance. $OVER holders should be able to vote directly on protocol changes, while the existing public proposal and discussion process preserves transparency and community review.

During the OIP discussion, community members raised valid concerns about moving directly to tokenholder voting without any additional safeguard. These concerns included whale concentration, temporary voting power accumulation, flash-loan-style governance risk, and the removal of an experienced review layer between tokenholders and major protocol decisions.

This proposal acknowledges those concerns and introduces an Elder veto mechanism as a compromise. The goal is to preserve direct $OVER-holder voting as the primary governance mechanism while retaining a limited emergency safeguard for extreme or clearly harmful governance outcomes.

This OIP also addresses concerns that protocol-controlled token balances could influence governance outcomes. Overtime treasury wallets and multisigs exist to manage protocol resources, not to participate as governance voters. Excluding treasury-controlled $OVER from voting helps ensure that direct Snapshot governance reflects community participation rather than internal treasury-held voting power.

## Specification

This OIP entails dissolving the Overtime Council and replacing Council voting with direct Snapshot voting by $OVER holders.

The proposed governance structure is:

1. Overtime Council is dissolved.
2. OIPs continue to follow the existing proposal flow:

   * GitHub proposal publication.
   * Discord discussion.
   * Snapshot vote.
3. Any community member may draft and propose an OIP.
4. If Snapshot posting requires privileged access, authorized team members or governance operators may post the vote on behalf of the proposer.
5. Voting power is based on $OVER token holdings according to Snapshot configuration.
6. Overtime treasury wallets, treasury-associated wallets, and treasury-controlled multisigs are not eligible to vote on OIPs using their $OVER token balances.
7. Regular OIPs require quorum equal to the lower of:

   * 10,000,000 $OVER voting power; or
   * 30% of circulating voting-eligible $OVER supply.
8. Meta-governance OIPs require quorum equal to the lower of:

   * 15,000,000 $OVER voting power; or
   * 50% of circulating voting-eligible $OVER supply.
9. Regular OIPs require at least 60% majority to pass.
10. Meta-governance OIPs require at least 80% majority to pass.
11. Council stipends are discontinued once the Council is dissolved.
12. Each Snapshot vote may be vetoed by at least 3 former Overtime Council members, acting as governance Elders, publicly vetoing it in Discord before the vote is considered final.
13. Active core contributors are excluded from acting as Elders for the purpose of this veto mechanism.
14. New Elders may be added if at least 3 existing Elders publicly approve the addition in Discord.

For the avoidance of doubt, $OVER held by the Overtime treasury, any treasury-associated wallet, any treasury-controlled multisig, or any other protocol-controlled wallet must not be used to vote on Snapshot proposals. Governance voting is intended to reflect the will of circulating, voting-eligible $OVER holders, not protocol-controlled token balances.

For quorum purposes, circulating $OVER supply means circulating voting-eligible supply. This excludes $OVER held by the Overtime treasury, treasury-associated wallets, treasury-controlled multisigs, and any other protocol-controlled wallets that are not eligible to vote.

Quorum is adaptive and should account for future reductions in circulating voting-eligible $OVER supply, including token burns. If 30% of circulating voting-eligible supply is lower than 10,000,000 $OVER, the regular OIP quorum should use the 30% circulating voting-eligible supply value. If 50% of circulating voting-eligible supply is lower than 15,000,000 $OVER, the meta-governance quorum should use the 50% circulating voting-eligible supply value.

In formula terms:

* Regular OIP quorum = min(10,000,000 $OVER, 30% of circulating voting-eligible $OVER supply).
* Meta-governance OIP quorum = min(15,000,000 $OVER, 50% of circulating voting-eligible $OVER supply).

Treasury-controlled voting power should therefore be excluded from quorum calculations and approval thresholds wherever Snapshot configuration allows.

The Elder veto safeguard is intended to address concerns raised during the OIP discussion around direct tokenholder voting, whale concentration, temporary vote accumulation, and the removal of the Council as a governance review layer.

The role of Elders is not to replace tokenholder voting or recreate the Council under a different name. Direct $OVER-holder voting remains the primary governance mechanism. Elders only provide a limited emergency safeguard against proposals that are clearly malicious, rushed, governance-breaking, or otherwise harmful to the protocol.

The veto should be treated as an exceptional protection mechanism, not a regular second approval layer. It exists to preserve a final human review layer in cases where tokenholder voting may be vulnerable to concentration risk, temporary voting power accumulation, unclear proposal wording, or other governance edge cases.

Active core contributors are excluded from acting as Elders so that the core team cannot use the veto mechanism to block proposals single-handedly. This ensures that the Elder veto remains an independent safeguard rather than a tool for core-team control over governance outcomes.

New Elders may be added over time if at least 3 existing Elders publicly approve the addition in Discord. This allows the Elder set to remain active and relevant without recreating a formal elected Council or ongoing stipend-based governance structure.

For the avoidance of doubt, this OIP changes the approval mechanism for OIPs. It does not remove the existing public proposal and discussion process.

## Rationale

This proposal follows the same governance maturity path already reflected in earlier governance changes.

The Council was first useful because Thales was early, technically active, and required frequent protocol decisions. Later, as proposal volume decreased, the Council was reduced from 7 members to 5. Stipends were then reduced to reflect lower governance activity.

Moving to direct $OVER-holder voting is the logical next step.

It improves governance by:

* Giving $OVER holders direct decision-making power.
* Removing coordination friction around Council voting.
* Reducing unnecessary governance costs.
* Preserving the public OIP discussion process.
* Keeping higher thresholds for meta-governance changes.

The quorum requirements are designed to require meaningful participation while remaining practical as $OVER supply changes over time. A fixed quorum alone could become too restrictive if a large portion of token supply is burned. For that reason, quorum is defined as the lower of a fixed $OVER amount or a percentage of circulating voting-eligible supply.

For regular OIPs, quorum is the lower of 10,000,000 $OVER voting power or 30% of circulating voting-eligible supply. For meta-governance OIPs, quorum is the lower of 15,000,000 $OVER voting power or 50% of circulating voting-eligible supply. This preserves strong participation requirements today while ensuring governance does not become blocked in the future if circulating voting-eligible supply materially decreases.

The 60% regular proposal threshold avoids simple narrow-majority capture, while the 80% meta-governance threshold preserves stronger protection for governance rule changes.

Excluding Overtime treasury-controlled $OVER from voting is an important safeguard for direct tokenholder governance. The purpose of this OIP is to move decision-making power to $OVER holders, not to allow protocol-controlled wallets or multisigs to influence outcomes using treasury-held tokens. This also directly addresses concerns that the treasury may hold enough $OVER to reach quorum or materially affect proposal outcomes.

Treasury wallets and multisigs exist to manage protocol resources, not to act as governance voters. Removing treasury-associated voting power from Snapshot participation makes governance outcomes more legitimate, reduces centralization concerns, and ensures that quorum and approval thresholds reflect actual community participation.

The Elder veto mechanism is included as a compromise between fully direct tokenholder governance and the concerns raised during community discussion. While this OIP removes the Council as the default approval body, it does not ignore the risk that direct token voting can be influenced by large holders, temporary voting power, or low-participation governance events.

By requiring at least 3 former Council members to publicly veto in Discord, the mechanism keeps a lightweight safeguard without preserving the current Council structure. It also ensures that any veto is transparent, attributable, and socially accountable. Elders cannot silently block governance outcomes, and a single individual cannot override tokenholder votes.

This approach preserves the main objective of the OIP — moving final governance power to $OVER holders — while adding a practical review layer for extreme cases. It gives the protocol protection against obvious governance abuse without continuing the operational burden, recurring elections, and stipend structure of the current Council model.

Excluding active core contributors from the Elder role is important because the veto mechanism is intended to be an independent governance safeguard, not a core-team control mechanism. The core team may continue to contribute to proposals, operations, Snapshot posting, and public discussion where needed, but it should not be able to block passed Snapshot votes through the Elder veto.

Allowing new Elders to be added with approval from at least 3 existing Elders gives the safeguard continuity without turning it into a new election-based Council. This keeps the mechanism flexible while preserving its limited scope.

## Implementation

Implementation is expected to be governance and operations focused.

The governance operators should:

1. Update Snapshot governance settings to reflect direct $OVER-holder voting.
2. Configure Snapshot to exclude Overtime treasury wallets, treasury-associated wallets, treasury-controlled multisigs, and any other protocol-controlled wallets from voting power where possible.
3. Configure regular OIP quorum as the lower of 10,000,000 $OVER voting power or 30% of circulating voting-eligible $OVER supply.
4. Configure meta-governance OIP quorum as the lower of 15,000,000 $OVER voting power or 50% of circulating voting-eligible $OVER supply.
5. Configure regular proposal approval at 60% majority.
6. Configure meta-governance approval at 80% majority.
7. Discontinue Overtime Council stipend payments.
8. Establish and publicly document the initial Elder set.
9. Document the public Discord veto process.
10. Update public governance documentation to reflect the new process.
