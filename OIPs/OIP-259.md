## Simple Summary

Extend the existing futures default-round underwriting model to specific-threshold high-odds parlays and system bets, shifting them from upfront escrow collateralization to payout-on-claim, thus improving capital efficiency, liquidity, and user experience.

## Abstract

Overtime V2 currently fully collateralizes every parlay and system bet at placement. While this guarantees constant onchain solvency, it also leads to significant unnecessary inefficiencies such as locking large amounts of liquidity for outcomes that are in many cases mutually exclusive or extremely unlikely to materialize.

Futures markets already operate under a default round payout-on-claim model, which has proven effective at unlocking capital without compromising solvency. This proposal introduces the same model for:
* Very high odds parlays above configurable thresholds: underwritten by the default round at settlement, without full upfront collateralization.
* Very high odds system bets above configurable thresholds: underwritten by the default round at settlement, without full upfront collateralization.

This enables significantly better capital utilization and far superior UX while maintaining payout guarantees through protocol-level controls and monitoring.

## Motivation

### 1. Parlay Scaling Limitation

Overtime Parlays and their compounded odds:
* Are highly profitable for LPs due to accumulated vig
* Are currently capped (200x max odds)
* Could have more competitive cap if potential payout wasn’t locked on trade execution

Example:
* $10 bet at 10,000x odds → requires $100,000 collateral
* At scale → millions locked unnecessarily

Additionally:
* Many parlay outcomes are mutually exclusive
* Full collateralization overestimates actual risk by a significant margin

This results in:
* Artificial odds caps
* Poor UX vs Web2 sportsbooks
* Reduced competitiveness

### 2. System Bet Scaling Limitation

System bets combine multiple parlay permutations into a single ticket, which can push the aggregate maximum payout very high even when individual combinations are modest. Under the current model:
* Full collateralization of the theoretical maximum payout overstates realistic risk, since only a subset of permutations can win in any given outcome scenario.
* Liquidity is locked across many mutually exclusive outcome paths simultaneously.
* Odds and stake caps must be set conservatively to account for worst-case payouts that are statistically improbable.

The effect mirrors the parlay case: capital is locked against payouts that will almost never materialize in aggregate, constraining both user offering and LP efficiency.

### 3. Precedent: Futures

Futures markets already operate under this exact model, with payouts sourced from the default round at claim time. This approach has:
* Freed substantial liquidity that would otherwise be locked for months against long-tail outcomes
* Maintained solvency through protocol-level risk controls
* Demonstrated that payout-on-claim underwriting is viable at production scale

Extending the same mechanism to high-odds parlays and high-odds system bets is a natural next step, applying a proven pattern to the next-largest source of idle locked capital in the protocol.

## Specification

1. High-Odds Parlay Handling
Parlays meeting configurable thresholds (set by pDAO, e.g. ≥ 10 legs, ≥ 50x odds):
* Will not have potential payouts locked in escrow on trade execution
* Will be settled via default round on claim if won

2. High-Odds System Bet Handling
System bets meeting configurable thresholds (set by pDAO, e.g. based on combination count, maximum potential payout, or effective odds):
* Will not have the maximum potential payout locked in escrow on trade execution
* Will be settled via default round on claim if won

3. Default Round Role
The default round will continue to act as the backstop liquidity pool for this class of trades,  already covering futures, and extended by this proposal to also cover very high odds parlays and system bets.

4. Risk Controls
Controls that, when combined, will maintain solvency guarantees with the new implementation:
* Max exposure limits per market
* Adjustable odds caps
* Per-user / per-market limits
* Real-time monitoring of any potential liabilities
* Ability to pause markets if thresholds are exceeded

## Rationale

This approach aligns with how traditional sportsbooks operate and allows Overtime to increase competitiveness with said platforms:
* Not all outcomes are collateralized simultaneously because full escrow lockups overcompensate for solvency guarantees.
* Risk is managed at portfolio level, not per-ticket.

Key advantages:
* Massive capital efficiency gains
* Improved liquidity availability enabling more trading activity
* Higher odds offering enabling better UX versatility
* Significantly better UX for parlay and system bet users
* Increased LPs and Protocol Fee performance

For LPs
* Higher capital utilization
* Increased turnover
* More profitable bet types (parlays, system bets)

For Users
* Higher max odds
* Better pricing
* More competitive offering vs Web2

For Protocol
* Reduced idle capital
* Improved scalability
* Stronger market-making capabilities

## Implementation
1. Introduce new flag for:
* High-odds parlay classification
* High-odds system bet classification
2. Update underwriting logic:
* Skip full collateral lock
* Track exposure separately
3. Route payouts:
* Default round at claim
4. Add monitoring dashboards + alerts
5. Governance-configurable thresholds

## Conclusion

This proposal significantly improves Overtime’s capital efficiency and competitiveness by eliminating unnecessary collateral lockups for high-odds parlays and system bets, building on the default round underwriting model already proven in futures markets.

By extending the payout-on-claim model to these additional ticket types, the protocol can:
* Unlock liquidity
* Scale its offering
* Deliver a UX comparable to leading Web2 sportsbooks

All while maintaining strong safety guarantees through controlled risk management.
